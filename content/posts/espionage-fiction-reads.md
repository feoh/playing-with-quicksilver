Some #espionage fiction reads I've enjoyed lately:

David McCloskey is a former CIA analyst, and I've read and enjoyed a few of his books:

- [Damascus Station](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/56769571-damascus-station)

  This was the first of his books I read. It introduces Artemis Proctor—a towering, stupendous badass of a spy whose favorite management tool is her Mossberg shotgun.

- [The Persian](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/228581146-the-persian)

  This was a change of scene for McCloskey—the main plot centers on a Mossad operation involving a spy in Tehran. I really enjoyed it!

- [London Station](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/241905153-london-station)

  I think I enjoyed this book a lot more than other reviewers did, and I suspect I know why. In some respects, it's less a traditional espionage read and more a work of dark fiction examining the train wreck a Trump or Trump-like administration can cause in the US intelligence community—and, perhaps equally importantly, the damage it can do to long-standing relationships with intelligence partners we've traditionally been very happily in bed with.

And one last book by M.P. Woodward:

- [The Handler](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/58936421-the-handler)

  I enjoyed this book, but it all felt a bit clichéd and... trope-y? The characters were reasonably well developed, and the overall story was perfectly fine, but I just felt like the net result didn't push me over the top, as some other books have.
